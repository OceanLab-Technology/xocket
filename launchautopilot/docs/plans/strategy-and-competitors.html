Build the launchpad that proves AI visibility

CodeHype, ScrollLaunch and MediaFast all sell “rank on Google and ChatGPT”. None of them measures whether it worked. The opening is a launch directory that tracks every product’s AI mention rate before and after, then helps makers earn the third‑party mentions that AI engines actually cite.

CodeHype

A six-week-old solo project with the best agent plumbing of the three: llms.txt, a 175 KB llms-full.txt, a public REST API and an MCP server with no login. Its traction claims contradict each other (“100,000 visitors this month”, “447+ products” against 279 in its own API). Its own Product Hunt launch drew 3 upvotes. Paying does not skip review, which is a fair point against ScrollLaunch.

ScrollLaunch

The strongest domain of the three, built in about five months on a reciprocal loop: free makers keep their dofollow link only while a ScrollLaunch badge stays on their site. Around it sit thousands of faceted “best”, “alternatives” and “vs” pages that reshuffle the same ~880 products. Paid listings take 16 of the top 25 slots this week. These are the exact patterns Google’s spam policies name.

MediaFast

Not a directory or PR tool. It tells founders what to post on Reddit and where. The premise has some support: Reddit is Perplexity’s most cited domain. The $1,999 tier runs warmed-up accounts to “seed” threads, which is managed astroturfing, and its free checker reports an “estimated rank position”, a metric the research below shows is noise.

Evidence rewards off-site mentions and punishes shortcuts

Most evidence here is vendor-published and correlational. The one controlled study is from 2023.

  • Content enrichment works. In the GEO paper, adding quotations raised visibility in AI answers by 41%, statistics by 33% and cited sources by 28%. Keyword stuffing lowered it 9%. Lower-ranked sites gained most (+115% for a rank‑5 site), which is where indie makers sit.
  • Mentions beat links. Across 75,000 brands, web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664, backlinks at 0.218 (Ahrefs).
  • Each engine prefers different sources, and preferences move. ChatGPT leans on Wikipedia and media; Perplexity and AI Overviews on Reddit and YouTube (Profound). ChatGPT’s Reddit share fell from about 60% to 10% within weeks in September 2025 (Semrush).
  • Review sites matter. Domains on several review platforms averaged 4.6–6.3 ChatGPT citations against 1.8 for those absent (SiteUp).
  • AI answers are unstable. Over 2,961 runs, the same brand list came back under 1% of the time and the same order under 0.1% (SparkToro via SEJ). API and in-app ChatGPT answers shared only 24% of brands (Surfer).

Launch and ranking logic that can’t be bought or gamed

Weekly cohorts, capped by editors, not by payment

Cohorts run Monday to Sunday UTC. Weekly suits a young community better than daily. Every launch passes editorial review (target 24 hours), and payment never changes review state. When demand exceeds the cap the queue is first-in-first-out with a visible estimated date. The paid tier picks a date among open slots; it never creates extra slots.

Score, not raw votes

  • Weighted votes. Weight comes from account age, verified sign-in (GitHub, Google, LinkedIn), engagement history and distance from the maker in the social graph.
  • Substantive comments. Minimum length, not from the maker, scored by a classifier with human spot checks.
  • Real clicks. First-party redirect clicks, one per session.
  • Return visits to the product page after launch.
  • Shrinkage toward the cohort mean, so 12 lucky votes can’t beat 60 real ones.

Anti-gaming

  • Vote-velocity anomaly detection against the cohort baseline.
  • Graph clustering to catch vote rings and maker-to-maker upvote swaps.
  • Device, IP-range and disposable-email checks.
  • Suspicious votes silently get zero weight, so cheaters get nothing to tune against.
  • Confirmed manipulation removes the product, logged on a public enforcement page.

Sponsored rows sit in fixed, labelled slots outside the leaderboard and carry rel="sponsored".

The GEO engine: audit, sample, estimate, recommend, prove

1. Audit

A rendering crawler checks what the evidence supports. Every finding is tagged strong, moderate or hygiene-only, so llms.txt and schema stay in their place.

  • robots.txt and the CDN allow OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, Bingbot and Googlebot. Training bots such as GPTBot can be blocked separately.
  • Pages are indexed in Google, Bing (which feeds ChatGPT and Copilot) and Brave (which overlaps heavily with Claude’s results).
  • Pricing, audience and integrations appear in server-rendered visible HTML.
  • “Last updated” dates reflect real changes.
  • Key pages carry statistics, quotations and cited sources.

2. Prompt sets, frozen before anything changes

15–100 prompts per product covering “best X for Y”, “alternatives to [competitor]” and comparison intents. They are drafted by an LLM from the product’s facts, edited by the maker, then frozen and versioned. One in five are control prompts the planned work shouldn’t affect, which separates real lift from engine-wide drift.

3. Sampling

Each prompt runs 5–10 times per engine per period, because one run is noise. The default channel is official APIs with web search: OpenAI, Perplexity Sonar, Gemini with Google grounding, Claude with web search. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode have no API, so that data comes from a licensed vendor after legal review. Every result records its channel, model version, locale and raw answer, and the dashboard labels which channel produced each number.

4. Estimation

An extraction model lists brands mentioned, sentiment, cited URLs and whether the product was recommended. Alias matching against the product’s facts checks it, and people audit 2% of samples. The headline metric is mention rate with a 95% interval. Share of voice, cited domains, sentiment and a position distribution are secondary.

Sample size, said plainly: at about 200 answers per period, a 20% mention rate carries roughly ±5.5 points. Detecting a lift from 10% to 20% needs about 200 answers per period, for example 25 prompts × 8 runs on one engine. Smaller panels show direction, not proof, and the product says so.

5. Recommendations and proof

The citation data answers the question makers care about: which sites do the engines cite for my prompts, and am I on them? The gap list names them, per engine: “Not on G2”, “Missing from listicle X (author contact)”, “Thread Y is active and on topic”. Each completed action is logged with a date. The report compares at least two weeks of baseline with the post window, treatment against control prompts, and gives a plain verdict: significant lift, no detectable change, or not enough data. Publishing the nulls honestly is what makes the positives believable.

Our own domain earns authority from original data, not link loops

Google’s March 2026 core update reportedly cut sites built on unedited template pages by 50–90%, and the August 18–21 spam update hit one site with 1.5M mostly programmatic URLs across the whole domain. The ScrollLaunch recipe is the thing to avoid.

Do

  • rel="sponsored" on anything tied to payment; a followed link only on listings an editor approved; ugc on comments
  • Badges earned for results (“Top 5, week 41”), never required for a link
  • Publish category pages only above a data threshold (e.g. 8+ reviewed listings with live AI data); noindex the rest
  • A monthly public AI Recommendation Index per category, with methodology and intervals
  • Human-edited write-ups from verified facts
  • Measure our own mention rate for “where to launch” prompts with the same engine

Don’t

  • Sell followed links or DR guarantees
  • Require a badge in exchange for a dofollow link
  • Generate country × tech × year page grids
  • Sell AI-written “stories” about customers (site reputation abuse risk)
  • Put “recommend us” instructions in llms.txt
  • Claim traffic we can’t show; run a public analytics dashboard instead

Growth for the platform itself: launch on Product Hunt and Hacker News, newsletter signup at account creation, the free audit as the lead magnet, founder build-in-public, and one sharp differentiator people repeat. Ours is verified AI-visibility lift.

How listed products get ranked: five compliant channels

  1. The product page. Visible facts first: pricing, audience, integrations, alternatives, reviews from people who clicked through, and a comparison block. Accurate SoftwareApplication, Organization with sameAs, Offer and Review markup behind it.
  2. Category and comparison pages. Earning a spot on a curated “best X for Y” page puts the product on the page type AI engines fetch for sub-queries.
  3. Review and profile sites. A done-with-you package sets up 10–20 profiles the citation data says matter (G2, Capterra, AlternativeTo, niche directories), replacing “100+ directory” blasts.
  4. Listicle and newsletter outreach. Contact discovery and pitch templates for authors whose pages the engines already cite for the product’s prompts. No paying hosts to publish.
  5. Community. The disclosed playbook below.

Distribution: a trusted newsletter and community without sockpuppets

The weekly newsletter carries the cohort’s top launches, one “AI visibility movers” story from the index, and one labelled sponsor. BetaList grew weekly sponsorships from $50 to $1,500; There’s An AI For That prices its premium listing on its newsletter. Short YouTube demo cuts of top launches help too, because YouTube is heavily cited by Perplexity and AI Overviews.

Makers connect their own Reddit account through Reddit’s official OAuth. The assistant finds relevant threads, checks each subreddit’s rules, drafts in the maker’s voice with “I built this” disclosure, and publishes on schedule once the maker approves each item. Rate caps, per-subreddit cooldowns, one-maker-per-thread and an account health score pause posting at the first removal or warning.

It never buys, warms or pools accounts, evades bans, votes, coordinates makers in a thread, or posts without approval. That is the direct counter to MediaFast’s $1,999 tier. The full rules are on the Growth Engine Logic page.

Architecture: Postgres core, engine adapters, a stats layer

A TypeScript monorepo (pnpm + Turborepo) scaffolded with Xocket:

  • apps/web: Next.js, server-rendered directory, dashboard and public index.
  • apps/worker: Node workers on a durable job system (pg-boss for the MVP; Trigger.dev, Inngest or Temporal later).
  • Postgres (Neon or Supabase) with Drizzle; R2 or S3 for raw answers and crawl snapshots; ClickHouse once Growth customers generate ~19,000 answers each a month.
  • Stripe for billing, Resend plus a newsletter platform, and Plausible with a public dashboard.

Risks

  • AI provider terms. We couldn’t verify current terms on collecting answers from the chat apps. Default to official APIs, use licensed vendors for in-app data only after legal review, and label every metric by channel. API answers differ from what users see, and the product has to say so. Check each provider’s terms before publishing aggregated outputs in the public index.
  • Directory drift into thin pages. A quarterly review noindexes or merges pages below the data and traffic threshold.
  • Makers misusing the community assistant. Disclosure templates, ratio guidance and no multi-account support reduce it, not eliminate it.
  • Honest results will include nulls. Many reports will say “no detectable change”. Publish them; pooled across customers they become the first real dataset on what works.
  • Copycats. Any competitor can bolt on a tracker. The defensible assets are the longitudinal dataset, the public index and a trusted newsletter, not the code.
  • Unverified numbers. No independent traffic or DR data exists for any competitor, engine-level data for Claude, Copilot and Gemini is thin, and the pricing above is a hypothesis to test.
